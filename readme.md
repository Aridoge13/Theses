# Academic Theses

This repository contains my undergraduate and postgraduate research theses in biotechnology and genomic science. These projects reflect my early research experience in microbial genomics, comparative genomics, molecular biology, phylogenetics, and conservation genetics.

The repository preserves the original academic work and provides an accessible record of the research conducted during my bachelor's and master's degrees.

## Theses

### 1. Genomic Study of Leprosy-Causing Bacteria

**Degree:** B.Sc. Biotechnology  
**Institution:** Amity University, India  
**Year:** 2020  
**Supervisor:** Dr. Prabuddha Gupta

This thesis investigated genomic and evolutionary differences between *Mycobacterium leprae* and *Mycobacterium lepromatosis*, two bacterial species associated with leprosy.

The work involved comparative genomic analysis and protein modelling to examine pathogen-associated differences. It also explored human host genetic variation, including susceptibility-associated regions, in relation to the geographic distribution of *M. lepromatosis*.

**Research areas:** Comparative genomics, microbial genomics, protein modelling, host–pathogen genetics.

> Note: The thesis was completed in 2020. Any reference to 2019 in the original document is a date error.

### 2. Phylogenetic Characterization of Indian Gaur Using the Cytochrome *b* Gene

**Degree:** M.Sc. Genomic Science  
**Institution:** Central University of Kerala, India  
**Period:** March–August 2022  
**Supervisor:** Dr. M. Nagarajan

This thesis examined the genetic diversity and phylogenetic relationships of Indian gaur (*Bos gaurus*) using the mitochondrial cytochrome *b* gene.

Samples were collected from four Indian states. The study involved DNA extraction, PCR amplification, sequence analysis, genetic-diversity estimation, and phylogenetic comparison with related bovine species.

The analysis provided a preliminary assessment of mitochondrial diversity and evolutionary relationships among Indian gaur and other members of the *Bos* genus, contributing to the broader understanding of gaur population history and conservation.

**Research areas:** Phylogenetics, mitochondrial genetics, population genetics, wildlife genomics, conservation genetics.
